# Fundamental Investment Concepts

Investing involves allocating capital to asset classes with the expectation of generating future returns through appreciation, dividends, or interest, while bearing associated market risk.

### Core Asset Classes
1. **Equities (Stocks)**: Ownership shares in public companies; offers high historical long-term growth potential accompanied by short-term price volatility.
2. **Fixed Income (Bonds)**: Debt securities issued by governments or corporations; provides regular interest payments and lower volatility relative to stocks.
3. **Cash & Cash Equivalents**: Treasury bills, certificates of deposit (CDs), and high-yield savings; offers capital preservation with inflation risk.
4. **Real Assets & Real Estate**: Physical property and REITs; provides income generation and inflation hedging.

### Diversification & Asset Allocation
- **Diversification**: Spreading capital across diverse sectors, geographies, and asset classes reduces unsystematic (company-specific) risk.
- **Time Horizon**: Longer investment horizons allow investors to ride out market cycles and harness compound growth.
- **Cost Basis & Rebalancing**: Periodically reviewing asset allocation ensures portfolio risk stays aligned with investor goals.
